Rajista Pay · built on NEAR

The shop prices in naira. The customer pays from any chain. The shop gets USDC on NEAR.

A till issues an invoice, shows a QR code, and the customer pays with USDC or USDT from Solana, Base, Ethereum, Tron, BNB Chain or NEAR. NEAR Intents converts it, and an invoice contract on NEAR decides whether it settles.

How a sale works

1

Ring it up in naira

The cashier adds items in ₦ and the rate. The till creates the invoice on NEAR with a till key, so there is no wallet prompt per sale. The item list is hashed into the invoice, so it cannot change later.

2

Customer scans and pays

They pick the coin and chain they already hold. NEAR Intents 1Click quotes the exact USDC the invoice needs and gives one deposit address. Anything sent above that comes back to them.

3

The contract settles it

The USDC arrives with the invoice id attached. The contract marks it paid and credits the shop. The till turns green. The shop withdraws to its wallet when it likes, and exports a CSV for the books.

Rules the contract enforces

Not a server. The invoice contract checks every payment that arrives.

  • Only real USDC can pay
  • An invoice is paid once
  • Short payments never settle
  • Cancelled and expired invoices refuse wallet payments and refund them
  • A paid invoice can never be cancelled or changed
  • Only the shop can withdraw its money
  • The till key can issue and cancel invoices, never move money
  • Cross-chain money is never bounced back into the network: a late or second payment is held and shown to the shop as a refund owed

Why

Many Nigerians who earn online keep their money in USDT or USDC, on whichever chain their employer or exchange uses. Taking that at a shop counter today means a screenshot, a wallet address typed by hand, and trusting that the right amount arrived on the right chain. Rajista Pay gives the shop one QR code per sale, a fixed price in naira, money that lands in one place on NEAR, and a record the shop's accountant can open.

Rajista Pay is from Rising Technology, a computer shop in Jaba, Kano, and the makers of the Rajista point-of-sale app.
